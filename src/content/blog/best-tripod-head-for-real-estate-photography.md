faqs:
  - q: Is a geared head better than a ball head for real estate photography?
    a: >-
      Yes, a geared head is better than a ball head for real estate interiors,
      because each knob moves one axis in fine steps, so you can straighten
      verticals without knocking the level out. A ball head is faster to aim,
      but loosening it frees every axis at once and the camera can sag after you
      tighten it.
  - q: How much load capacity does a real estate tripod head need?
    a: >-
      A real estate tripod head needs a load capacity about three times your
      camera, lens and plate. A full-frame DSLR with a 17-40mm zoom weighs about
      1.25 kg, so a 4 kg head covers it and a 6 kg head such as the Benro GD3WH
      or K&F GD3W leaves room for a heavier body and an L-bracket.
  - q: Do I need an Arca-Swiss compatible head for real estate?
    a: >-
      You need an Arca-Swiss compatible head if your camera already wears an
      Arca-type plate or L-bracket; the Benro GD3WH, K&F GD3W and Arca-Swiss d4
      take it directly. The Manfrotto 410 and 405 use the 410PL plate and the
      XPRO Geared uses the 200PL, so you either carry those plates or add an
      Arca clamp adapter.
  - q: Is the Manfrotto 410 Junior still worth it?
    a: >-
      The Manfrotto 410 Junior is still worth it if you want a proven geared
      head and accept its 410PL plate and 1.22 kg weight. Owners call it
      reliable, while APALMANAC warns its rubber knobs and gears can wear over
      time; the Benro GD3WH gives a similar workflow on an Arca-type plate.
  - q: Is the Arca-Swiss d4 worth it for real estate?
    a: >-
      The Arca-Swiss d4 is worth it for a full-time architecture or real estate
      photographer who wants one head for years: it carries 30 kg at 980 g with
      dual panning. For a new real estate business, the Benro GD3WH covers a
      typical interior kit for far less, and the d4's Amazon listing ships
      without a plate.

**The right tripod head for real estate photography follows your plate system first and your kit weight second.**

## Geared vs ball tripod head for real estate photography

**A geared tripod head for real estate photography beats a ball head because it moves one axis at a time in fine steps.** With a ball head, loosening the knob frees every axis. Lifting the lens a degree can add roll, and the frame can settle after you lock it, so straight verticals drift. A DPReview owner describes a budget ball head that "drops about a centimeter after you tighten it," which undoes a leveled interior. ## Tripod head for real estate photography problems owners run into, and the fix

**Most problems with a tripod head for real estate photography come from plates and clamps, not the gears.**

- **The quick release plate does not fit the head.** Manfrotto's 410PL and 200PL plates do not fit Arca-type clamps. Pick one system for every body, or add an Arca clamp on top of the Manfrotto plate as DPReview owners do. Our explainer on [whether you need an Arca Swiss plate](/blog/what-is-an-arca-swiss-plate-and-do-i-need-one/) covers the choice.
- **The camera twists on a budget plate.** Swap the stock plate for a 70 mm Arca-type plate with grippy material, as Nathan Cool recommends.
- **The clamp knob breaks.** Do not over-torque two-stage knobs like the Benro's; tighten, then lift the rig by the camera to check.
- **Leveling by the legs takes forever.** A BCG Forums member calls it "an exercise in pain and frustration"; set the legs roughly, then level with the head's roll and pitch knobs.
