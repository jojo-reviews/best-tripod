## Crossbow tripod comparison by height, weight and rest type

**A crossbow tripod is chosen by rest type first (clamp or yoke), then by the height you shoot from.** A gun clamp holds the bow on its own; a yoke only cradles it. Legs are aluminum or carbon fiber, and each tripod head pans so you can follow a whitetail.

## Crossbow tripod clamp or yoke

**A clamp crossbow tripod holds the bow hands-free for a long sit, while a yoke lets you lift the crossbow off in a second.** That is the real choice, and I'd settle it before comparing weights.

A clamp suits the youth hunter and long waits. Crossbow Magazine's tester picks a clamp tripod for youngsters who cannot hold a crossbow up for long, and calls a stand-mounted BOG rest a route to benchrest accuracy. The cost is speed. A Crossbow Nation owner who hunts from blinds chose a yoke-style rest because, as he puts it, "the Deathgrip having the weapon secured by clamping jaws will not allow me to do that." A yoke, like the Primos or Vanguard, cradles the forend so you can swing or lift off fast.

## Crossbow tripod height for a ground blind

**A crossbow tripod for a ground blind must reach your seated window height, and a standing shot needs one of the 59 to 62 in tripods.** TenPoint built the Axis for seated blind shooting, which is why it stops at 40 in.

When I line up the maker ranges, they fall into three bands:

- **Seated only:** TenPoint Axis, 25 to 40 in.
- **Floor to standing:** BOG DeathGrip (both versions), 7 to 59 in.
- **Kneeling to tall standing:** Primos Gen3 Tall, 24 to 62 in; Vanguard Quest, 27 to 62 in.

My rule: measure from the blind floor to the middle of your shooting window while seated on your chair, then add the height of the clamp. BOG owners say real height runs a little under the listed 59 in, so a tall hunter shooting standing may prefer the 62 in sticks. For more on matching legs to your height, our [tripod buying checklist](/tripod-buying-checklist/) has the steps.

## How to set a crossbow on a tripod safely

**A crossbow sits safely on a tripod when the clamp or yoke holds the forend, clear of the limbs, cables and string path.** Shooting Sports Retailer warns that a rifle yoke "could interfere with crossbow string travel, with is very dangerous."

1. Set the legs on firm ground and level the head with its bubble.
2. Raise the tripod to your seated or standing window height before the bow goes on.
3. Clamp or rest the forend ahead of the trigger, behind the limbs, never touching the string or cables.
4. Check the jaws close tight on your forend; narrow or odd-shaped forends may need a tighter clamp.
5. Add tilt and pan tension so a butt-heavy crossbow stays level instead of tipping nose-up.
6. Dry-check your swing through the window, then load only when you are ready to hunt.

## Crossbow tripod problems owners run into, and the fix

**Most crossbow tripod complaints are bulk, a tipping bow, string clearance and clamp fit, and each has a fix I'd try before returning the tripod.**

- **Too bulky for a small blind.** One Axis owner says the DeathGrip in "a smaller blind can see it being difficult." Fix: the Axis, or set the DeathGrip legs at their narrowest angle in the corner.
- **The crossbow points at the sky.** A Crossbow Nation owner says "my xbow acts butt heavy n then likes to point to the sky." Fix: clamp nearer the balance point and tighten the tilt tension. Our guide to a [ball head that sags after tightening](/blog/ball-head-sags-after-tightening/) covers the same physics.
- **The rest touches the string or limbs.** Field & Stream notes BOG's standard Universal Shooting Rest yoke can interfere with some crossbow limbs. Fix: clamp the forend further back and check clearance before loading.
- **The clamp will not close tight.** Axis owners report the jaws do not close tight on some weapons. Fix: test with your crossbow at home and pick a clamp that fits your forend or rail. One Crossbow Nation owner built a ball head and weapon clamp rig on a carbon tripod that a friend used to take hogs past 100 yards.

> **From our notes:** The Crossbow Nation owner whose crossbow tipped nose-up in a DeathGrip changed how we built this guide. We had sorted the five by weight, like most top results. His complaint, and another owner's warning that an underrated ball head let his bow pivot down suddenly, made us put rest type and balance first. A heavy clamp tripod can still fail a crossbow if the bow is not clamped at its balance point.
