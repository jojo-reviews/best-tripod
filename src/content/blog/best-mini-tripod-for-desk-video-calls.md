faqs:
  - q: How high should a webcam or camera be for video calls?
    a: >-
      The lens should sit roughly at your eye level, or a touch above, when you
      are seated. A mini tripod placed straight on the desk usually holds the
      camera well below that, so most people raise it on books, a box or a riser
      to stop the camera looking up at their chin.
  - q: Can you put a webcam on a mini tripod?
    a: >-
      Yes, if the webcam has a 1/4 in-20 tripod thread in its clip or base,
      which many desk webcams do. All five tripods here use that standard
      screw, so a threaded webcam mounts directly without an adapter.
  - q: Do mini tripods hold mirrorless cameras?
    a: >-
      Some do. The SmallRig VT-20 is rated for 6 kg and the Neewer M521 for
      5 kg. The SmallRig BUT2664 head is rated for about 2 kg, and an owner
      write-up reports it held a Sony A7IV with a 24-70mm lens without creep.
      The Ulanzi MT-16 is reported to manage only 500 g in vertical mode, so it
      is a poor match for a mirrorless body.
  - q: Is a mini tripod better than a monitor-top webcam mount?
    a: >-
      A monitor-top mount puts a light webcam at screen height with no desk
      footprint, which is hard to beat for a webcam alone. A mini tripod wins
      when you use a phone or mirrorless camera, want to move the camera between
      rooms, or need a ball head to fine-tune the angle.
  - q: Which mini tripod has an Arca-Swiss plate?
    a: >-
      Three of these five ship with an Arca-type quick-release plate: the
      SmallRig VT-20, the Neewer M521 and the SmallRig BUT2664. One reviewer
      calls the BUT2664 plate size non-standard, so check that one against your
      existing clamps before relying on it.

The best mini tripod for desk video calls holds your webcam, smartphone or camera steady and aims it at your face, not up your nose.

I have been shooting since 2017, and video is one of the genres I work in, so I care about where a camera sits even when the "shoot" is just a Zoom call. My cameras are a Sony A7 IV and a Canon EOS R6 Mark II, and my support kit runs on ball heads and Arca-Swiss plates, so when I look at a desk tripod I ask the same questions I ask of any support: will it hold the camera, and will it put the lens where it needs to be?

I compared five tabletop tripods that keep coming up in mini tripod roundups: the [Ulanzi MT-16 Extendable Tripod](amzn:Ulanzi+MT-16+extendable+tripod+with+ball+head+2052) for most desks, the [SmallRig BUT2664](amzn:SmallRig+mini+tabletop+tripod+Arca-type+QR+plate+BUT2664) for a mirrorless camera used as a webcam, the [Manfrotto PIXI Mini](amzn:Manfrotto+PIXI+mini+tripod+black+MTPIXI-B) as the simplest desk stand, and two 50 cm tabletop tripods, the [SmallRig VT-20](amzn:SmallRig+VT-20+aluminum+mini+tripod+ball+head+4289) and the [Neewer M521](amzn:NEEWER+portable+travel+mini+tabletop+tripod+20+inch+50cm+M521), for more height. Everything below comes from maker and retailer specs, editorial reviews and the few owner reviews I found.

## Best Mini Tripods for Desk Video Calls: Quick Picks

**The Ulanzi MT-16 is the best mini tripod for desk video calls for most people, the SmallRig BUT2664 is the pick for a mirrorless camera, the Manfrotto PIXI Mini is the simplest low stand, and the SmallRig VT-20 or Neewer M521 gives the most height.** The three minis need a riser to get near seated eye level; the two 50 cm tripods get much closer.

- **Best budget extendable desk stand: Ulanzi MT-16 Extendable Tripod with Ball Head.** About 22 to 44 cm tall, 170 g, with a cold shoe for a mic or light.
- **Best for a mirrorless camera on the desk: SmallRig Mini Tabletop Tripod with Panoramic Ball Head (BUT2664).** All-aluminum, 2 kg ball head, Arca-type plate, about 9 in tall.
- **Best simple desk stand: Manfrotto PIXI Mini Tripod (MTPIXI-B).** Fixed at about 13.5 cm, rated for 1 kg, folds into a grip.
- **Best for raising the camera toward eye level: SmallRig VT-20 Aluminum Mini Tripod.** About 17 to 52 cm, 6 kg rating, Arca-type plate, about 700 g.
- **Best tall desk tripod with a bubble level: Neewer M521 Desktop Mini Tripod.** About 17 to 50 cm, 5 kg rating, Arca-type plate, about 700 g.

## Buying Guide: Choosing a Mini Tripod for Desk Video Calls

**I judge a mini tripod for desk video calls on height and load first, stability second, and Arca-Swiss only if you move a camera between supports.** All five are portable enough for a laptop bag. Here is what I check, in order.

### Reaching eye level without the center column

The fixed PIXI Mini and SmallRig BUT2664 both sit in the 13 to 23 cm range, the Ulanzi tops out around 44 cm, and the VT-20 and Neewer M521 reach about 50 to 52 cm with their center columns up. Only those two have a real center column, and even they may need a book underneath on a low desk. I'd raise the whole tripod on books or a box rather than angling the camera upward, because a camera tilted up from below gives the unflattering under-chin view. Eye level also saves your neck on long Zoom, Microsoft Teams or FaceTime calls. If you're unsure of your eye height, my guide on [how tall a tripod should be for your height](/blog/how-tall-should-a-tripod-be-for-my-height/) walks through the math.

### Load capacity for a webcam, phone or mirrorless camera

Weigh your camera with its lens, cage and mic on a kitchen scale and compare it with the lower rated figure, not the headline one. A rated load is the most the head can hold before it slips, so stay well under it; the lowest figure here is 500 g. A mirrorless body with a zoom points you toward the SmallRig and away from the Ulanzi's 500 g vertical limit. For a camera that also travels, see our [best travel tripod for mirrorless camera kits](/blog/best-travel-tripod-for-mirrorless-camera/).

### Head type, thread and Arca-Swiss plates

All five use the 1/4"-20 thread, the standard tripod screw on cameras and on many webcams. Check your webcam's clip or base for that socket before you buy. The SmallRig BUT2664, SmallRig VT-20 and Neewer M521 add an Arca-type plate. An Arca-Swiss plate is the dovetail standard used by most ball-head clamps, and it lets a camera slide from the desk tripod onto a full-size Arca-Swiss head without swapping plates. Because my own ball heads take Arca-Swiss plates, that is the feature I would weigh most if the camera also leaves the desk. Since one reviewer calls the BUT2664 plate size non-standard, check that one with your other clamps first.

### Stability and desk footprint

Tripod stability on a desk comes down to leg spread, weight and what is under the feet. The rigid SmallRig BUT2664 is the steadiest of the low minis, and the 700 g VT-20 and Neewer M521 add weight and a wider leg spread for a heavier camera; the Ulanzi gets less steady as its arm extends. Rubber feet or a mouse mat stop a tripod skating when you bump the desk.

### Mini tripod leg locks and adjustment

The PIXI Mini has no leg locks at all, the SmallRig BUT2664 sets its leg angle with a hex tool, the Ulanzi adjusts by extending its arm, and the VT-20 and Neewer M521 use twist locks on five leg sections. Our [tripod buying checklist](/tripod-buying-checklist/) covers locks in more depth.

## Webcam vs Phone vs Mirrorless on a Mini Tripod for Desk Video Calls

**The mini tripod for desk video calls you need depends on the camera: a webcam is the easiest to mount, a smartphone needs a clamp, and a mirrorless camera needs the strongest head and a careful cable run.** Here is how I'd pair each with these five:

1. **Webcam:** any of the three works if the webcam has a 1/4 in-20 thread; the Ulanzi gives the most height.
2. **Smartphone:** add a phone holder or clamp (the PIXI Mini has none) and use the Ulanzi's height or a riser. Our [phone tripod for vlogging guide](/blog/best-phone-tripod-for-vlogging-with-remote/) covers clamps and a Bluetooth remote.
3. **Mirrorless:** use the SmallRig BUT2664 on a riser, or the VT-20 or Neewer M521 for more height, and keep the USB or HDMI cable slack so it doesn't tug the head.

Lighting matters as much as the tripod: face a window or put a small light on the Ulanzi's cold shoe.
