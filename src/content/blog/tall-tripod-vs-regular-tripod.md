In the tall tripod vs regular tripod choice, a tall tripod wins if you are about 6 feet or taller, shoot on slopes or over fences, or work in wind; a regular tripod wins on weight, folded length and cost for everyone else. The deciding number is the height of the tripod legs alone, with the center column down.

I have been shooting since 2017, and my own support kit is carbon fiber tripods, ball heads and Arca-Swiss plates. The first thing I ask of any legs is how high they stand before the column comes up. Our chart of [how tall a tripod should be for your height](/blog/how-tall-should-a-tripod-be-for-my-height/) gives the target; here I compare the two classes.

## Tall Tripod vs Regular Tripod by the Numbers

**A regular tripod has legs that extend [50 to 63 inches](https://www.breathingcolor.com/blogs/news/how-to-buy-the-right-tripod), while a tall tripod reaches about 60 inches or more before the center column moves.** I use one verified example of each class:

| Spec | Regular: Manfrotto MT055XPRO3 | Tall: K&F Concept 90 inch |
|---|---|---|
| Height, center column down | 55 in (B&H) | [63.8 in](https://www.kfconcept.com/KF09.119_90-tall-overhead-aluminum-camera-tripod-monopod) (maker) |
| Maximum height, column up | 67 in | 90.6 in |
| Est. viewfinder, column down | 60 to 63 in | About 64 to 72 in |
| Weight | 5.5 lb, legs only | 4.46 lb with ball head |
| Folded length | 24 in | 21.3 in |
| Minimum height | 3.5 in | 26.2 in |
| Leg sections | 3 | 4 |

My viewfinder estimate adds 5 to 8 in for a tripod head and camera (nothing at the low end for the K&F, in case its 63.8 in includes the ball head). Eye level at 6 feet tall is about 67 to 68 in, so the regular tripod leaves a 6-foot shooter 5 to 7 in short.

## When a Tall Tripod Beats a Regular Tripod

**A tall tripod beats a regular tripod in four situations: a photographer of 6 feet or more, sloping ground, a view blocked by a fence or crowd, and wind.**

- **Height.** Stooping is a recurring complaint in DPReview threads; one member describes short legs as "requiring you to bend over to look through the VF". Legs of 60 in or more end that.
- **Slopes.** DPReview member Snap Happy explains why: "Longer legs can be extended down the slope, maintaining a higher working height."
- **Fences and crowds.** Extra leg length lifts the lens over them.
- **Wind.** The same member spreads the long legs wider and still keeps a usable height; see our fixes for [tripod shake in wind](/blog/how-to-stop-tripod-shake-in-wind/).

A tall tripod also keeps you off the center column, which a Photography Stack Exchange answer warns "can cause vibration and cost rigidity (especially in a windy environment)".

## What a Tall Tripod Costs in Weight, Folded Length and Stability

**Within one tripod family, the tall tripod is heavier, longer when folded and slightly less stiff: The Center Column found a long version should score [11% lower](https://thecentercolumn.com/explorations/tripod-stiffness-vs-height/) than its standard sibling at the same height.**

- **Weight.** "The big problem is weight," writes a DPReview member about his tall studio legs. Carbon fiber legs are the fix.
- **Folded length.** A member with a premium long tripod at about 70 cm folded calls it "basically to long to fly with". Measure your bag first.
- **Stability.** Budget tall tripods flip the trade: the K&F is lighter and shorter folded than the Manfrotto, but its four leg sections use 16 to 25 mm tubes and flex near full extension. Keep the thinnest section retracted.
- **Low angles.** The Manfrotto drops to 3.5 in for macro photography; the K&F stops at 26.2 in unless you reverse its column.

> **From our notes:** The 55 in figure for the Manfrotto MT055XPRO3 is what reframed this comparison for me. Its headline 67 in looks tall enough for anyone, but B&H's spec table puts only 55 in on the legs. The K&F Concept 90 inch carries 63.8 in on its legs. My takeaway: the two classes differ less in maximum height than in where the height comes from, the legs or the column.

## Which Tripod Height Should You Get, Tall or Regular?

**Get a tall tripod if your height minus 10 to 12 inches is more than the legs-only height of the regular tripod you are considering.**

- Under about 5 ft 10 in, flat ground: a regular tripod.
- 6 feet and over, or slopes and fences: a tall tripod, used short of full extension.
- Frequent flights: whichever folds to fit your bag.
