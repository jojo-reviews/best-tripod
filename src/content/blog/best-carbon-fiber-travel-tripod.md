faqs:
  - q: What is the best carbon fiber travel tripod?
    a: >-
      The best carbon fiber travel tripod for most travelers is the Peak Design
      Travel Tripod (Carbon Fiber): 2.8 lb, 15.5 in folded, rated to 20 lb and
      51.25 in tall with the column down. For a DSLR with a 70-200mm I'd pick
      the Gitzo Traveler Series 1 kit instead.
  - q: How much lighter is a carbon fiber travel tripod than aluminum?
    a: >-
      A carbon fiber travel tripod is about 0.6 lb lighter than the same design
      in aluminum: B&H lists the Peak Design Travel Tripod at 2.81 lb in carbon
      and 3.44 lb in aluminum, roughly 18% less. Digital Camera World puts the
      typical carbon saving at around 25%.
  - q: What is the best travel tripod for a DSLR?
    a: >-
      The best travel tripod for a DSLR is a carbon model with a high load
      rating and good column-down height, such as the Gitzo Traveler Series 1
      kit (22 lb, 55.3 in column down) or the HEIPI W28S (44 lb). Keep the
      column down and mount long lenses by their collar.
  - q: Will a carbon fiber travel tripod fit in a carry-on?
    a: >-
      Yes, all six carbon fiber travel tripods here fold to 13 to 17.7 in, short
      enough for most carry-on bags; the SIRUI Traveler 5C (13 in) and 3 Legged
      Thing Leo 2.0 (14.7 in) are shortest. Check your airline's size rules and
      measure the inside of your bag.
  - q: Are 5-section carbon travel tripods less stable than 4-section ones?
    a: >-
      Yes, a 5-section carbon travel tripod is usually less stiff than a
      4-section one of the same size, because its thinnest leg tube is smaller
      and it has one more joint. It folds shorter, so extend the thickest
      sections first and leave the thinnest one in when you can.

## Carbon fiber travel tripod comparison by weight, fold, height and load

**A carbon fiber travel tripod should be judged on four numbers: weight with the head, folded length, height with the center column down, and the lowest load rating in the system.** Most lists stop at max height, but column-down height is the height you can use without losing stiffness. All six picks fall in the 50 to 64 in max-height band and ship with a ball head.

## The 6 best carbon fiber travel tripods, reviewed

**Each carbon fiber travel tripod below fits one kind of trip, from ounce-counting city breaks to a DSLR with a telephoto.** The video in this section is Dustin Abbott's review of the HEIPI W28S, which he compares with the Peak Design.

### Peak Design Travel Tripod (Carbon Fiber): best carbon fiber travel tripod overall

The Peak Design Travel Tripod (Carbon Fiber) is a five-section carbon fiber travel tripod from Peak Design with its ball head built into the column, and it's my overall pick. B&H lists it at 2.8 lb, 15.5 in folded and 20 lb of load, with a 5.5 to 60 in range. Fstoppers' spec table gives 51.25 in with the column down. The legs pack around the column to about the width of a water bottle, which is why the [Peak Design Travel Tripod](amzn:Peak+Design+Travel+Tripod+Carbon+Fiber) slips into bags where round-legged tripods don't.

For my kit, a full-frame body and a 24-70mm, that 20 lb rating leaves plenty of room. A PetaPixel writer who bought one calls the legs the highlight but says his biggest complaint is the head: it wasn't the easiest to lock down securely. A Reddit owner in r/peakdesign reports an angle leg lock that broke in half, and a long-term reviewer wishes for more than two leg angles. I'd buy it for carry-on trips with a zoom up to 70-200mm.

- **Who it's for:** carry-on travelers with mirrorless or DSLR bodies and standard zooms.
- **Skip it if:** you want a separate pan lock for panoramas.

### Gitzo Series 1 Traveler Carbon Fiber Tripod Kit (GK1545T-82TQD): best carbon fiber travel tripod for a DSLR

The Gitzo GK1545T-82TQD is a four-section carbon fiber travel tripod kit from Gitzo, pairing GT1545T legs with the GH1382TQD center ball head. Gitzo lists 1.45 kg (3.2 lb), 42.5 cm (16.7 in) folded, 163.5 cm (64.4 in) fully up and 140.5 cm (55.3 in) with the column down, plus a 10 kg (22 lb) safety payload. That column-down height is the best here, and four sections mean the thinnest tube is still 14.7 mm. Its reverse-folding legs wrap around the head, each with a twist lock, and the bare legs take other heads on their 1/4-in or 3/8-in screw.

Gitzo itself recommends the Traveler for [135mm lenses (200mm max.)](https://www.gitzo.com/us-en/tripod-traveler-series-1-4-sections-gt1545tus/), so this is the tripod I'd choose for a DSLR with a 70-200mm, but not for my 200-600mm. The head has an independent pan lock and takes an Arca-Swiss plate, but has no friction control. Owners on B&H call it sturdy yet light enough to carry, while several say the kit head isn't up to the legs and a few find it flimsy fully extended. Keep the column down and the [Gitzo Traveler kit](amzn:Gitzo+Lightweight+Series+1+Traveler+Carbon+Fiber+Tripod+with+Center+Ball+Head+GK1545T-82TQDUS) is the steadiest pick here.

- **Who it's for:** DSLR and full-frame shooters with lenses up to 200mm.
- **Skip it if:** you need friction control on the head.

### SIRUI Traveler 5C Carbon Fiber Tripod: lightest carbon fiber travel tripod

The SIRUI Traveler 5C is a five-section carbon fiber travel tripod from SIRUI, and at 1.87 lb (0.85 kg) with its head it's the lightest full-height pick. SIRUI lists 13 in (33 cm) folded, 54.3 in max, 6.3 in minimum and 8.8 lb (4 kg) of load. The Amazon listing names an Arca Swiss quick-release plate. SIRUI publishes no column-down height, so expect to raise the column for eye-level shots.

That 8.8 lb rating fits a mirrorless body and a 24-70mm, my most-used travel zoom, but not a DSLR with a fast telephoto. TechGearLab found it held up well in drops but wasn't very confidence-inspiring in wind and quite wobbly fully extended. On First Quadcopter, an owner who loves its weight replaced the basic ball head a week after buying. I'd pick the [SIRUI Traveler 5C](amzn:SIRUI+Traveler+5C+carbon+fiber+tripod) when grams matter more than stiffness.

- **Who it's for:** ounce-counting mirrorless travelers with a short zoom.
- **Skip it if:** you shoot long exposures in wind.

The HEIPI W28S is a five-section carbon fiber travel tripod from HEIPI whose center column comes out as a separate tabletop tripod. I like it for a full-frame body with a 70-200mm: the rating is high and the head locks with a lever. ## Which carbon fiber travel tripod should you get?

**Choose a carbon fiber travel tripod by your heaviest lens first, then by bag size.**

1. Carry-on trips with a mirrorless or DSLR and a zoom up to 70-200mm: Peak Design Travel Tripod (Carbon Fiber).
2. DSLR or full-frame with a 70-200mm and long exposures: Gitzo Traveler Series 1 kit.
3. Lightest bag, mirrorless and a short zoom: SIRUI Traveler 5C.
4. Full-frame kit plus a tabletop tripod in one: HEIPI W28S.
5. Heavy gear, low angles or a monopod on the same trip: 3 Legged Thing Leo 2.0.
6. Best value with good column-down height: NEEWER LT32.

## Is a carbon fiber travel tripod worth it over aluminum?

**Yes, a carbon fiber travel tripod is worth it if you carry it far, because it's lighter and damps vibration better; aluminum makes sense when the tripod rides in a car.** The cleanest comparison is one design in both materials: B&H lists the Peak Design Travel Tripod at [2.81 lb and 3.44 lb](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/explora/photography/news/travel-tripod-reinvented-the-peak-design-travel-tripod) in carbon and aluminum. That's 0.63 lb saved, about 18%, on an otherwise identical tripod. Digital Camera World puts the usual saving at around 25%.

Carbon fiber here means carbon-fiber reinforced polymer: carbon fibers bonded in resin, which gives better vibration damping than aluminum (aluminium in UK spec sheets) tubes of the same weight. Carbon also feels warmer in cold hands. The catch is price and how it fails: carbon can crack on a hard hit where aluminum dents. Our [carbon fiber vs aluminum tripod for hiking guide](/blog/carbon-fiber-vs-aluminum-tripod-for-hiking/) sorts that trade by trip.

## Carbon fiber travel tripod load ratings for a DSLR or full-frame kit

**A carbon fiber travel tripod holds only as much as its weakest part, so compare the head rating and the maker's lens guidance, not the leg rating alone.** The camera lens usually sets the load more than the body, because a fast zoom lens sits far forward of the head. The NEEWER LT32 shows why: 22 lb legs, 10 lb head. Gitzo skips the load race and recommends 135mm lenses, 200mm at most, on its Traveler.

- **Mirrorless and a 24-70mm:** any pick works; the SIRUI 5C's 8.8 lb is the floor.
- **DSLR or full-frame with a 70-200mm:** Gitzo, Peak Design or HEIPI; mount the lens by its collar.
- **Long telephoto, 200-600mm class:** none of these, in my view; a full-size tripod is the safer call.

A rating is a ceiling, not a working load. Stiffness drops as you raise the column or open the thinnest sections.

## How to set up a carbon fiber travel tripod for sharp shots in wind

**A carbon fiber travel tripod stays sharp in wind when the column stays down, the thickest leg sections carry the height and extra weight sits low.**

1. Open the thickest leg sections first and leave the thinnest ones in.
2. Keep the center column down; reach height with the legs.
3. Spread the legs to a wider angle on rough or windy ground.
4. Hang your bag from the column hook so it just touches the ground.
5. Mount long lenses by the lens collar, centered over the legs.
6. Fire with a 2-second timer or a remote.

## Carbon fiber travel tripod problems owners run into, and the fix

**Most carbon fiber travel tripod complaints come from the center column, thin fifth sections, small heads and short column-down height.**

- **Wobble with the column up.** A DPReview forum member warns: "The center column make the Travel Tripod less stable so you should not extend it." Pick the Gitzo (55.3 in) or NEEWER (52 in) if you need height without it.
- **Thin five-section legs.** Another DPReview member says "I would stay away from 5 section legs." The Gitzo and NEEWER use four; on five-section models, leave the last one in. Our [4 vs 5 section tripod guide](/blog/4-section-vs-5-section-tripod/) explains the trade.
- **Small heads that creep.** Owners of the SIRUI 5C, Peak Design and Gitzo all name the head as the weak spot. Lock it, then check it again before a long exposure.
- **Bending over.** One DPReview reply notes "a travel pod will be shorter requiring you to bend over to look through the VF". Taller shooters should skip the Leo 2.0.
- **Wind on a light tripod.** TechGearLab found the 5C shaky in wind; hang weight from the hook and go lower.
