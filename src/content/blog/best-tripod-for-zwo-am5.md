The best tripod for ZWO AM5 is ZWO's own [ZWO TC40 Carbon Fiber Tripod](amzn:ZWO+TC40+2-Section+Carbon+Fiber+Tripod) if you travel with a small refractor, because the mount bolts straight on and it weighs 2.3 kg. If you image with a C8 or a long refractor, I would move to steel legs or a pier: the [iOptron LiteRoc Tripod](amzn:iOptron+LiteRoc+Tripod+CEM40) for a backyard SCT, or the [iOptron Tri-Pier](amzn:iOptron+Tri-Pier+for+GoTo+Mounts+8034) for the heaviest loads.

The ZWO AM5 is a harmonic equatorial mount: strain wave gearing lets it carry a telescope with no counterweight, so the tripod decides how steady it stays and how well it holds polar alignment. This is research from spec sheets and owner reports, not testing. For smaller mounts, see our guide to the [best tripod for telescope mounts](/blog/best-tripod-for-telescope/).

## Tripod for ZWO AM5 Comparison by Height, Weight and Load

**The right tripod for ZWO AM5 imaging clears your scope's weight with room to spare at the base height you need; the TC40 is the lightest and the Tri-Pier carries the most.** ZWO rates the AM5 at [13kg without the counterweight and 20 kg with it](https://www.zwoastro.com/2022/01/13/am5-faq-35-things-you-want-to-know-about-zwo-am5-mount/), so I compare each rating against that 20 kg.

| Tripod | Base height | Weight | Rated load | Load vs 20 kg | How the AM5 attaches |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ZWO TC40 | 470-800 mm | 2.3 kg | 50 kg | 2.5x | ZWO plate, 3 M6 screws + knob |
| INNOREL RT90C | Up to 1,600 mm | 2.72 kg | 40 kg | 2x | 3/8" screw on Innorel plate |
| iOptron LiteRoc | 765-1,168 mm | 6.8 kg | Not published | – | Own 3/8" bolt or PE200 |
| iOptron Tri-Pier | 795-1,095 mm | 11.6 kg | 100 kg | 5x | PE200, 2 M6-16 screws |
| Celestron Heavy Duty CPC | 965-1,422 mm | 12.7 kg | Not published | – | PE200 on center rod |

The TC40 with the 200 mm ZWO PE200 pier extension reaches about 1,000 mm, mid-range for the LiteRoc and Tri-Pier. Stability costs carrying weight: the Tri-Pier adds 9.3 kg over the TC40 for twice the rated load.

## The 5 Best Tripods for the ZWO AM5, Reviewed

**Each AM5 tripod below fits one kind of setup, from a carry-on rig to a heavy SCT in the backyard.**

### ZWO TC40 Carbon Fiber Tripod: Best tripod for travel with the AM5

The [ZWO TC40 Carbon Fiber Tripod](amzn:ZWO+TC40+2-Section+Carbon+Fiber+Tripod) is a 2-section carbon-fiber reinforced polymer tripod from ZWO, made for its AM3, AM5 and AM7 mounts. The AM5 bolts on with three M6 screws and a center 3/8" knob, so it is the only pick that needs no adapter. It weighs [2.3 kg](https://agenaastro.com/zwo-tc40-carbon-fiber-tripod.html), folds to 500 mm and is rated for 50 kg, with a spreader, spikes and a weight bag in the box.

Owners split by scope size. One runs a 17 lb refractor on it happily; another writes, "I have the carbon fiber tripod and it just isn't steady enough for my C6 and C8 for imaging." The base tops out at 800 mm, so I would add the PE200 for long tubes.

### INNOREL RT90C Carbon Fiber Tripod: Best taller carbon tripod for the AM5

The [INNOREL RT90C Carbon Fiber Tripod](amzn:INNOREL+RT90C+Carbon+Fiber+Bowl+Tripod+40mm) is a 4-section carbon fiber bowl tripod from Innorel with 40 mm top tubes, rated for 40 kg. AM5 owners call it a bigger TC40: the mount and the PE200 drop into its top and lock with one knob. With all four sections out it reaches 160 cm; a minenko.org comparison found it 557 g heavier than the TC40, mostly from those sections.

The catch is the plate. One owner's AM5 tilted in the cup overnight because the ZWO plate is tapered: "Lesson learned: if you're using the Innorel tripod, use the plate supplied with the tripod." I would use Innorel's plate and check that the mount cannot rotate on its single screw.

### iOptron LiteRoc Tripod: Best steel tripod for an AM5 with a C8

The [iOptron LiteRoc Tripod](amzn:iOptron+LiteRoc+Tripod+CEM40) is a 2-section stainless steel tripod from iOptron with 1.75 in legs, made for its CEM40 and GEM45 mounts. It weighs 6.8 kg and lifts the base to 765-1,168 mm. A Cloudy Nights owner runs an AM5 with an EdgeHD 8 on it and finds it steadier than the TC40 even with the bag loaded.

Fitting it takes one part on the 3/8"-16 thread: "You do need to get your own bolt to go up through the middle, but it's trivial purchase from a hardware store." With the PE200 you remove the center pin instead. I'd pick it for a backyard or car-side SCT, not for a hike.

### iOptron Tri-Pier: Best AM5 tripod for heavy scopes and long refractors

The [iOptron Tri-Pier](amzn:iOptron+Tri-Pier+for+GoTo+Mounts+8034) is an aluminum portable pier on three legs from iOptron, rated for [220 lbs (100 kg)](https://www.highpointscientific.com/ioptron-tri-pier-for-select-ioptron-mounts-8034). That is five times the AM5's maximum payload, and the central pier keeps a long tube clear of the legs. The base runs 795-1,095 mm.

ZWO's forum staff confirm the PE200 bolts to it with two M6-16 screws. Cloudy Nights owners do exactly that, though one had a plate machined for a firmer fit. At 11.6 kg it is my pick for a C11 or a long refractor at home.

## How to Mount a ZWO AM5 on a Third-Party Tripod

**A ZWO AM5 goes on any tripod with a 3/8"-16 bolt, and the PE200 pier extension is the cleanest adapter for iOptron and Celestron tops.**

1. Check the tripod's center bolt: the AM5 base takes a 3/8"-16 thread.
2. On an iOptron top, remove the PE200's lower silver adapter and fix the PE200 with two M6-16 screws.
3. On a CPC tripod, unscrew the center rod, thread it up into the plate and lock the PE200 with the nut.
4. On an RT90C, bolt the AM5 to Innorel's own plate with its 3/8" screw.
5. Seat the AM5 on the PE200 and close its three levers, or tighten the center bolt.
6. Level the tripod, then twist the mount by hand to check nothing rotates.

## Tripod for ZWO AM5 Problems Owners Run Into, and the Fix

**Most complaints about a tripod for ZWO AM5 setups come down to shake with heavy scopes, wind, a low base and a slipping plate.**

**The TC40 shakes with a C6 or C8.** Load the weight bag and tighten the spreader first; one owner says it "also does better with the rock sack loaded up." If guiding RMS in arcseconds still jumps, move to the LiteRoc or the Tri-Pier.

**Wind ruins subs.** Hang weight low and keep leg extensions short; our guide to [stopping tripod shake in wind](/blog/how-to-stop-tripod-shake-in-wind/) covers windbreaks.

**The base is too low.** One owner explains, "I like the taller height of the Literoc which makes it easier for visual use and also for me to image lower horizon objects when they get close to the tree tops." Add the PE200 to the TC40, or pick a taller tripod.

**The mount tilts on an Innorel tripod.** Swap to the Innorel plate or a three-screw adapter.
