A tripod extension adds height to a tripod you already own in three ways: raising the built-in center column, screwing an add-on extension column or pole onto the top, or fitting a taller tripod head. All three lift the camera above the point where the tripod legs meet, so every added inch costs stability, and past a gap of about 12 inches a taller tripod is the better answer.

I have been shooting since 2017, and my support kit is carbon fiber tripods, ball heads and Arca-Swiss plates. I do the sums before I put anything on top of a tripod. Our [tall tripod vs regular tripod](/blog/tall-tripod-vs-regular-tripod/) comparison covers buying longer legs; this guide covers the legs you have.

## Tripod Extension Types Compared by Added Height

**Of the three ways to extend a tripod, the add-on extension column adds the most height: the Sirui SL-200 measures [7.9 to 12.6 in](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/1605319-REG/sirui_sl200_carbon_fiber_center_column.html) from closed to open.**

| Type | Also sold as | Height it adds | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Built-in center column | Center post | Up to the length of your column | A gap of a few inches |
| Add-on extension column | Extension pole, tube or rod, tripod extender, center column extension | 7.9 to 12.6 in (Sirui SL-200) | A gap of 8 to 12 in with a light camera |
| Taller tripod head or riser | Head extension | Only its own height | The last inch or two |

An extension arm is a different tool: it reaches sideways for overhead shots and adds no standing height.

## How Stable Is a Tripod Extension Pole or Column?

**A tripod extension pole is less stable than the same height on the tripod legs, because it works like a raised center column: tester The Center Column measured [roughly 15% less stiffness](https://thecentercolumn.com/2021/02/01/why-center-columns-reduce-tripod-stability/) from fitting a column at all, and vibrations that decayed four times slower with the column fully extended.**

A camera above the apex sits on a lever arm, so wind or a shutter press becomes more torque where the legs meet. A load rating does not cover this: the SL-200's 26 lb says what the carbon fiber tube carries, not how fast vibration dies.

Wobble is the recurring complaint in forum threads on this question. In a Photo.net thread, member joel_jimenez1 warns that "extending the top pole is not a good idea. This will introduce wobble into your video. You want to extend from the bottom."

My fixes:

1. Extend every leg section first.
2. Raise your own center column a few inches.
3. Open an add-on column only as far as you need.
4. Fire with a self-timer or remote release.
5. Take the extension off in wind.

> **From our notes:** The 7.9 in closed length of the Sirui SL-200 changed how I read these products. A built-in column drops to zero when the wind picks up; an add-on column does not. Even closed, it holds my camera 7.9 in above the legs. So I think of it as a center column that is always raised, and I would unscrew it for long exposures.

## When a Taller Tripod Beats a Tripod Extension

**A taller tripod beats an extension when the gap to eye level is more than about 12 inches, when you shoot in wind, or when the camera and lens are heavy.**

Find your gap: your height minus 10 to 12 in, minus your legs' height with the column down.

- **A few inches short:** use the column you have, within our [center column up or down](/blog/tripod-center-column-up-or-down-for-landscapes/) limits.
- **About 8 to 12 in short, light camera, calm air:** an add-on extension column.
- **More than 12 in short, wind or a heavy lens:** longer legs. The Photo.net poster had a 4 ft tripod and wanted 5 to 6 ft, a 12 to 24 in gap that no single add-on column covers. My 200-600mm wildlife lens would stay off an extension too.
