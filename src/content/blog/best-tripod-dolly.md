The best tripod dolly is the one that fits your tripod's feet, then your rig's weight. For round or rubber feet I pick the [SmallRig 3986](amzn:SmallRig+3986+Quick-Lock+tripod+dolly), a 3.97 lb folding dolly with 3 in braked wheels and a 33 lb rating. For twin-spiked cinema legs, the [Proaim Lineo](amzn:PROAIM+Lineo+heavy+duty+tripod+floor+dolly) is the cup-style floor dolly I would buy. I ranked six from maker specs and owner reviews, not hands-on use.

## Tripod dolly comparison by load, wheels and foot type

**A tripod dolly is a camera dolly for the tripod you own, chosen on foot type, load capacity and wheel size; these six run from 33 lb to 1,100 lb.** If you are still choosing the legs, our [beginner fluid head video tripods](/blog/best-video-tripod-with-fluid-head-for-beginners/) come first.

This table compares the six on maker and retailer specs:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| NEEWER Photography Tripod Dolly | Round or rubber | 50 lb | 6 lb | 3 in, braked | Floor |
| K&F Concept MS30 | Round or rubber | 44 lb | 5.7 lb | 3 in, locking | Floor |
| Manfrotto 114 | Large round feet | 132.28 lb | 14.29 lb | 127 mm, braked | Floor |
| Proaim Lineo | Twin spiked | 1,100 lb | 23.5 lb | 100 mm PU, locking | Floor, track option |
| Glide Gear SYL 960 | Not stated | 35 lb | Not published | Casters plus track wheels | Floor and track |

The weight column matters: the Lineo weighs about six times the SmallRig, and the Manfrotto 114 needs a [44.1 in working footprint](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/553954-REG/Manfrotto_114_114_Heavy_Duty_Cine_Video.html), so the heavy bases stay in the studio. All six fold; the three clamp dollies under 6 lb are the ones to carry on location.

## How I picked these tripod dollies

**I kept tripod dollies with a published load rating, a wheel spec and readable owner reports.** Specs come from maker pages and B&H spec tables; owner notes from B&H, maker review pages and forums.

> **From our notes:** The Manfrotto 114's foot type reshaped this list. Some buying lists file it as a dolly for spiked video feet, but B&H lists the 114 for round feet and sells the 114MV for spiked feet. One suffix decides whether your legs sit in it at all. So I sorted by foot type first, then load, and listed each dolly's own weight, because the heavy bases weigh 14 to 24 lb before you add a tripod.

## The 6 best tripod dollies, reviewed

**Each tripod dolly below suits one foot type and rig weight.**

### SmallRig 3986 Universal Tripod Dolly: best tripod dolly for most photo and hybrid rigs

The SmallRig 3986 is a folding aluminum tripod dolly from SmallRig, and my pick for round-footed photo and light video tripods. SmallRig lists a 15 kg (33 lb) payload, 1.8 kg (3.97 lb) of weight and legs that extend from 298 to 435 mm, and its 3-inch wheels have individual brakes. Its clamps take legs up to 54 mm thick. B&H owners like how small it folds and how it glides on smooth floors. One wished the arms reached wider, and another found his tripod feet pulled away from the clamps when he raised the tripod. I'd skip it for a heavy cinema head.

### NEEWER Photography Tripod Dolly Heavy Duty 50lb: best budget tripod dolly for heavier rigs

The [NEEWER Photography Tripod Dolly](amzn:NEEWER+Photography+Tripod+Dolly+Heavy+Duty+50lb+3+inch+wheels) is a heavy-duty folding tripod dolly from NEEWER, and my budget pick when the rig is heavier. NEEWER rates the 3 in version at 50 lb, the most of the clamp dollies here, for 6 lb of dolly. Each metal ball-bearing caster has its own caster brake, and NEEWER says the locks stop the wheels from swiveling as well as turning. Legs run 10.7 to 15.75 in, so a wide-stanced tripod may need its legs drawn in. Owners call it beefy and steady. Buy the 3 in model: a 2 in version is rated for 33 lb.

### K&F Concept MS30 Photography Tripod Dolly: best tripod dolly for repeat setups

The [K&F Concept MS30](amzn:K%26F+Concept+MS30+tripod+dolly+44lbs) is a folding aluminum tripod dolly from K&F Concept, my pick if you reset the same tripod spread every session: its 2-section legs carry scale markings, so you return to a width by number. K&F lists a 20 kg (44 lb) load, 2.6 kg (5.7 lb) of weight, 3 in silent wheels on metal ball bearings and a 38.5 cm (15.2 in) fold, the shortest here. One K&F reviewer found the clamps stiff at first; a Kentfaith reviewer would keep it indoors, away from grit.

### Manfrotto 114 Cine/Video Deluxe Dolly: best tripod dolly for large round-footed video legs

The [Manfrotto 114](amzn:Manfrotto+114+Cine+Video+Deluxe+Dolly) is a heavy-duty aluminum video dolly from Manfrotto for large still and video tripods, and my studio pick. Manfrotto lists a [60 kg (132.28 lb) safety payload](https://www.manfrotto.com/global-en/video-movie-heavy-dolly-114/), 127 mm individually braked wheels, a cable guard at each wheel and 6.48 kg (14.29 lb) of weight. B&H lists it for round feet; spiked legs need the 114MV. B&H owners praise its stability; complaints are its weight, guards that drag on carpet, and a width that may not clear doorways under 36 in.

### Proaim Lineo Portable Video Camera Floor Dolly: best tripod dolly for spiked cinema legs

The Proaim Lineo is a portable aluminum floor dolly from Proaim for heavy tripods with twin spiked feet, and my pick for cinema legs and jib stands. Proaim rates it for [500 kg (1,100 lb)](https://www.proaim.com/collections/tripod-dolly/products/proaim-lineo-portable-video-camera-floor-dolly) and lists 10.7 kg (23.5 lb), 100 mm polyurethane wheels with individual locks, and legs set 28 in apart. Proaim says the floor wheels swap for its track wheels, and owners call it built to last. Proaim also confirms some spike designs, such as the FreeBlazer AD-Pro8, do not fit its cups.

### Glide Gear SYL 960 Hybrid Tripod/Track Dolly: best tripod dolly for floor and track moves

The [Glide Gear SYL 960](amzn:Glide+Gear+SYL+960+tripod+dolly+floor+track) is a hybrid floor-and-track tripod dolly from Glide Gear, and my pick if you plan to add track. It comes with four locking floor casters and four urethane track wheels for 1 to 2 in track, built from 6061 aluminum and rated for 35 lb. The legs extend another 5 in for wider tripods. ## Which tripod dolly should you get?

**Pick a tripod dolly by your tripod's feet first, then by rig weight and where you roll it.**

- Round-footed photo or hybrid tripod: SmallRig 3986, or the K&F MS30 for marked legs.
- Heavier video rig on a budget: NEEWER Photography Tripod Dolly, 3 in version.
- Big round-footed studio legs: Manfrotto 114.
- Twin-spiked cinema legs or a jib: Proaim Lineo.
- Straight moves on track later: Glide Gear SYL 960.

## How to fit your tripod on a tripod dolly

**Fit a tripod dolly by matching the foot type, setting its legs to the tripod's working spread, then locking every wheel.** The video in this section shows a SmallRig dolly set up and rolled on hardwood.

1. Check your tripod feet: round or rubber feet go in clamps, twin spikes need cups.
2. Open the tripod to the angle you shoot at and measure from the center to one foot.
3. Extend the adjustable legs of the dolly to that reach and lock them.
4. Seat each foot, tighten the clamp around the foot, or drop each spike into its cup.
5. Mount the camera, then lock every wheel before you touch the head.
6. Roll the dolly on a smooth floor, slowly, pushing low on the legs, never from the head.

A tripod dolly lifts the whole rig by its wheel and frame height, so the centre of gravity rises and the tripod tips more easily on a fast push or a seam. Keep the centre column down and spread the legs as wide as the dolly arms allow.

## Tripod dolly problems owners run into, and the fix

**Most tripod dolly complaints come down to jitter, wandering casters, hard wheels on seams and feet that slip.**

- **Shaky, noisy rolling shots.** A recurring complaint in r/videography threads; one poster adds that "the sound of the wheels is distracting." Use 3 in or larger rubber wheels, move slowly on hard floors and record sound separately.
- **Casters wander at the start.** Another r/videography poster notes "the wheels always swivel until they get rolling." Free-swivel casters point wherever they stopped, so the first inches drift. Swivel-only locks would hold two casters parallel, but none of these clamp dollies has them (NEEWER's locks stop the roll too). Push an inch in the direction of travel before you record so each caster trails in line, or run track wheels on dolly track.
- **Hard wheels chatter on seams and tile joints.** A B&H owner says the SmallRig's wheels vibrate on tile, and Cloudy Nights posters found 3 in casters chatter on concrete and stall on grass. Softer rubber or polyurethane wheels ride over grout lines more quietly, though Glide Gear owners note soft wheels add rolling resistance. Spin each wheel and rock it sideways before a shoot: play in the bearing shows up as jitter. Tape cables flat or use the Manfrotto 114's cable guards.
- **Feet slip out of the clamps.** Tighten each clamp after the tripod is at shooting height, strap each leg to its dolly arm if a foot still moves, and on spiked legs ask the maker whether your spike shape fits.
- **Extra weight on shoots that move a lot.** Photo assistants on r/Photoassistants call dollies added weight when the set keeps moving. I'd keep a dolly for shoots that stay in one room.

## Tripod dolly vs slider: which gives smoother moves?

**A slider gives the smoother move for short, straight shots, while a tripod dolly rolls the whole tripod between setups or along a smooth floor.** A slider sits on the tripod and runs the camera along a fixed-length rail, so the move repeats exactly but ends where the rail ends. A tripod dolly goes any distance, but seams and floor texture reach the shot. For long straight moves, track wheels on dolly track, as on the Glide Gear SYL 960 or Proaim Lineo, give you both.
