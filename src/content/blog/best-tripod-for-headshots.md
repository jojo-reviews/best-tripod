In headshot photography, a tripod for headshots has one job: hold the lens at your subject's eye level for a whole session. The best tripod for headshots, for most studios, is the [Feisol CT-3442 Tournament Rapid](amzn:Feisol+CT-3442+Tournament+Rapid+carbon+fiber+tripod) with Feisol's optional column. If your clients run tall, I'd take the [Vanguard VEO 3+ 263AB](amzn:Vanguard+VEO3+263AB160S+aluminum+tripod+ball+head), the only pick that reaches an average man's eye level on its legs. Our guide on [how tall a tripod should be for your height](/blog/how-tall-should-a-tripod-be-for-my-height/) has the eye-level math. I compared five tripods on maker specs and owner reports; we have not used them ourselves.

## How I picked these headshot tripods

**Each headshot tripod here earned its place on legs-only height, column travel and owner reports of stiffness with a portrait lens.** I started from the tripods headshot specialists name most: Headshot Crew's gear list, Alan Ranger's guide, Improve Photography and Scott Lawrence's video.

Headshot Crew also lists Feisol's CT-3472LV, which has a levelling center column. A levelling (leveling) column sits on a short half-ball, so you level the camera by loosening one knob instead of adjusting three legs. I chose the CT-3442 because I found no reliable new US listing for the LV (nor for Vanguard's VEO 3+ 303CBS).

> **From our notes:** Feisol's 55.12 in legs-only figure for the CT-3442 made me redo the whole ranking around subjects, not photographers. Scott Lawrence wants one setup to cover clients from 5 ft to 6.5 ft, an 18 in spread. No column here covers it from one leg setting; the Feisol's comes closest at 13.38 in. That is why the picks below are matched to the kind of client list you shoot.

## The 5 best tripods for headshots, reviewed

**The Feisol CT-3442 Rapid is the best tripod for headshots in a studio, and each other pick wins one setup: lights, tall clients, budget or location.**

### Feisol CT-3442 Tournament Rapid 4-Section Carbon Fiber Tripod: best tripod for headshot studios

The Feisol CT-3442 Tournament Rapid is a four-section carbon fiber tripod from Feisol, and one of the two Feisol models Peter Hurley's Headshot Crew lists. Feisol rates this exact model to 55.12 in without a column and 68.50 in with its optional column kit, at 2.51 lb and a 55 lb load. For headshots, I'd buy the column for those 13.38 in. A DPReview owner calls it stiff and light and says it holds a 70-200mm telephoto zoom lens with almost no vibration. The same owner found the apex screws loose and says the fine leg threads grind with grit. It ships as legs only, so pair it with a tripod head such as the Arca-Swiss ball head Headshot Crew suggests.

### Manfrotto 055 Carbon Fiber 4-Section Tripod with Horizontal Column (MT055CXPRO4): best headshot tripod for a lit studio

The Manfrotto 055 Carbon Fiber 4-Section Tripod is a full-size carbon tripod from Manfrotto, and my pick when lights and reflectors crowd the set. It stands 55.12 in with the column down and 66.93 in up, with a 20 kg safety payload. Its Easy Link socket takes a reflector or LED arm, and the 90-degree column swings horizontal with the camera on. B&H owners praise how small it folds for its size and how secure the flip locks feel. One owner thinks the weight saving over the aluminum 055 isn't worth the cost.

### Vanguard VEO 3+ 263AB Aluminum Tripod with VEO BH-160S Ball Head: tallest headshot tripod on its legs

The [Vanguard VEO 3+ 263AB](amzn:Vanguard+VEO3+263AB160S+aluminum+tripod+ball+head) is a three-section aluminum tripod kit from Vanguard with a dual-axis BH-160S ball head, and my pick for tall clients. Vanguard lists 58.4 in with the column down and 68.4 in up, with a 33 lb rating. Its multi-angle column swings from 0 to 180 degrees without resetting the legs. ## Lens height for headshots, worked out for short and tall subjects

**A headshot tripod should put the lens at the subject's eye level, roughly 4 to 5 in below the top of their head.** A DPReview member measured about 170 mm (6.7 in) from top plate to viewfinder for a ball head and DSLR, so I add 6.7 in.

On the legs alone, the steadiest setting:

- Vanguard VEO 3+ 263AB: about 65.1 in, the eye level of a subject near 5 ft 9 in. CDC's measured averages for US adults are [68.9 in for men and 63.5 in for women](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/fastats/body-measurements.htm), so it suits the average man without the column.
- Befree GT XPRO, Feisol and 055: about 61.8 to 62.6 in, for a subject near 5 ft 6 in.
- MT190XPRO4: about 59.9 in, for a subject near 5 ft 4 in.

With the column fully up, the Feisol and Vanguard reach about 75 in at the lens, enough for a 6.5 ft client. The MT190XPRO4 stops near 69.7 in (a 6 ft 2 in subject). Load won't limit any of them: Canon's RF70-200mm F2.8 L IS USM Z weighs [about 2.5 lb](https://www.usa.canon.com/shop/p/rf70-200mm-f2-8-l-is-usm-z), well inside even the Befree's 26.46 lb rating.

## Tripod heads and columns for headshots: ball, pistol-grip or geared

**A ball head suits most headshot tripods, because one knob frees every axis for the small tilts a posing session needs.** Headshot Crew pairs its Feisol picks with an Arca-Swiss monoball.

- **A pistol-grip or joystick head**, such as the Manfrotto 322RC2, is a ball head you unlock by squeezing a grip. A headshot photographer at 1kCreatives says his budget grip head would "loosen, the camera would slip", and fixed his Manfrotto by setting its friction adjustment.
- **A geared head or geared column** moves in small crank turns, so height and angle change without the camera dropping.
- **A studio column stand** on wheels goes higher and moves faster than any tripod, but it stays put; portrait photography on location needs legs that fold.

## How to set up a tripod for headshots

**Set the legs for your typical client, frame once, then work from beside the camera.** Headshot Crew recommends a focal length in the [85mm to 100mm range](https://headshotcrew.com/gear) on full frame, or a telephoto zoom such as a 70-200mm; both flatter faces more than a wide-angle lens up close and put the tripod a few steps back.

1. Spread the legs at the first leg angle and extend the thickest sections first.
2. Set the lens at a stand-in's eye level and check the bubble level.
3. Lock the ball head for consistent framing, leaving a little friction to tilt a few degrees.
4. Set up tethering to a laptop on a tether table clamped to a tripod leg, or use a remote trigger, and step beside the camera.
5. Between clients, move only the column; touch the legs when it runs out.

## Headshot tripod problems owners run into, and the fix

**Most headshot tripod complaints come down to posture, mixed client heights, a short tripod or a slipping head.**

- **Bad posture.** Scott Lawrence says that without a tripod "my neck, back, and shoulders would be a complete mess". Set lens height so you stand upright.
- **Clients of different heights slow the workflow.** For corporate headshots, one r/AskPhotography reply says: "I use a tripod with a geared column and head so I can quickly adjust the height and angle". Pick a long column and keep a posing stool.
- **Shooting from too low.** Legs under 55 in leave you bending; the viewfinder belongs at your own eye level when standing.
- **A head that slips mid-session.** Set the head's friction, or see our fix for a [ball head that sags after tightening](/blog/ball-head-sags-after-tightening/).
