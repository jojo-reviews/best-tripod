To clean tripod legs after the beach, I rinse them with fresh water before they collapse, flush the salt out with warm water at home, strip the leg locks only when they feel gritty, then dry everything and grease the threads lightly. It works for twist locks and flip locks.

## Cleaning tripod legs after the beach, in short

**Tripod legs need a fresh-water rinse after every beach shoot and a strip-down only when grit gets inside the locks.**

1. Rinse the extended legs before you collapse them.
2. At home, pull the feet off and flush the leg sections with warm water.
3. If the legs grind or feel stiff, take the locks apart.
4. Wash the threads and shims with dish soap and a toothbrush.
5. Fully extend the legs and dry them for a full day.
6. Grease the threads lightly, reassemble and test.

The same habit matters when choosing a [landscape photography tripod for seascapes](/blog/landscape-photography-tripod/): legs you can strip easily are legs you will actually clean.

## Rinse tripod legs at the beach before you collapse them

**Tripod legs that go into the bag sandy carry the grit straight into the locks.** One Stack Exchange owner had to strip his legs a week later: "I think my mistake was collapsing the tripod while still dirty, bad idea." So I keep the legs extended until the sand is off.

- Knock loose sand off and blow out the lock joints.
- Pour fresh water from a jug over the legs while the salt is still wet; Ulanzi notes salt crystallizes as soon as it dries.
- Wipe the wet metal parts with a microfiber cloth.

## Flush salt out of the tripod legs with warm water at home

**A cold splash removes the sand you can see, but salt hides inside the lower tubes and under the feet.** An Induro owner on FM Forums flushed his legs in the tub and found the next day that "the interior walls of the lower legs had lot of salt caked to the sides". Hot water under pressure removed it.

- Pull off rubber feet and spikes and rinse underneath.
- Run warm water through each open section; Lincoln Harrison showers his Gitzo's upper legs for 5 minutes.
- Legs that won't come apart can be slid in and out under soapy water.

A carbon fiber tripod doesn't rust, but its collars, nuts and spikes can corrode, so I rinse it as carefully as aluminum.

## Take the tripod legs apart when they feel gritty

**A grinding sound or stiff collar means sand is inside the lock, and only a strip-down gets it out.** Ulanzi suggests every three to five beach sessions; ProMaster says [every 3 to 6 months](https://www.blog.promaster.com/home/2022/11/8/how-to-clean-tripod-saltwater) for frequent outdoor shooters. My rule: rinse always, strip when it grinds or the locks went into the surf.

1. Photograph each step, then unscrew one twist lock at a time.
2. Lay the parts out largest to smallest; SLIK and Really Right Stuff both keep each sleeve with its own section.
3. Brush warm, soapy water along the threads and shims with a toothbrush.
4. Rinse, then towel each part.

Wear gloves against carbon splinters; Visual Wilderness needed [about an hour](https://visualwilderness.com/equipment/how-to-clean-your-tripod) for two tripods. The video below shows a full strip-down.

| Part | Twist-lock legs | Flip-lock legs |
|---|---|---|
| Where sand hides | Collar threads and shims | Lever pivots, nuts, tube walls |
| What comes apart | Collar, shims, sections | Feet; levers only if screwed |
| What gets grease | Thin layer on the threads | Only what the maker specifies |

Our [twist lock vs flip lock comparison](/blog/twist-lock-vs-flip-lock-tripod-legs/) covers sand in more depth.

## Dry the tripod legs fully, then grease the threads lightly

**Tripod legs need a full dry before any grease goes on, or salty water stays sealed inside.** Ulanzi leaves the legs extended and the sections separated for [at least 24 hours](https://www.ulanzi.com/blogs/guides/clean-tripod-after-beach), open ends down.

For the lubricant, Really Right Stuff uses a thin layer of synthetic grease on the twist-lock grooves; Visual Wilderness uses lithium grease. Keep it off the leg tubes (Lincoln Harrison's rule) and never use WD-40 on threads (Ulanzi's). Grease also holds sand: one dune shooter wrote "The grease was a dust and sand magnet, and almost impossible to rid of sand once it was in there." I read that as use less, wipe the excess and clean sooner.

> **From our notes:** The FM Forums report of salt caked inside Induro legs changed this guide. We had planned a simple rinse-and-dry routine. Then one owner described a cold tub flush, an overnight dry, and salt still crusted inside the lower tubes the next morning. That is why we put warm water and feet-off rinsing ahead of grease, and why I'd check inside one lower section before calling a beach tripod clean.

## Tripod leg problems after the beach, and the fix

**Most tripod leg problems after the beach come from salt that dried or sand that stayed inside.**

- **White crust on flip-lock nuts.** A Manfrotto owner on FM Forums feared corrosion, then wrote "I thought it was salt corrosion, but it was just coated with salt." Soak and scrub first.
- **A seized section.** Ulanzi soaks it in warm fresh water for [10 to 15 minutes](https://www.ulanzi.com/blogs/guides/clean-tripod-after-beach) before twisting it apart.
- **Threads still gritty.** Owners in DPReview threads call stripping twist connections after sand "definitely a pain", but it is the fix: strip it again and replace scored shims.
- **A shim breaks on reassembly.** A Gitzo owner on DPReview warns "The grommets between legs are pretty fragile and it's hard to get replacements". I'd keep spares.

If a cleaned section still slips, our [tripod leg lock won't hold fixes](/blog/tripod-leg-lock-wont-hold/) go lock by lock.

## Keep tripod legs cleaner on the next beach shoot

**Tripod legs stay cleaner when the locks never touch sand or water.** Extend the lowest sections first so the joints sit above the splash line. Taped plastic bags over the lower legs keep sand out, and a Feisol owner on DPReview never retracts the legs until they are rinsed.

So for that salty tripod: rinse it open, flush it warm, strip it when it grinds, and dry it before greasing.
