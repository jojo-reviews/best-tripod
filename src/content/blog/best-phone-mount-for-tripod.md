The best phone mount for tripod use is a metal clamp that opens wide enough for your phone in its case and locks its rotation. For most people I'd pick the [Ulanzi ST-27](amzn:ULANZI+ST-27+metal+phone+tripod+mount): aluminum, three cold shoes, and an Arca-type base that also takes the standard 1/4"-20 screw, so it fits nearly any tripod. On a budget, the [Ulanzi ST-02S](amzn:ULANZI+ST-02S+aluminum+phone+tripod+mount) does the basics in metal. For a Pro Max in a thick case, the [Manfrotto MCPIXI](amzn:Manfrotto+Universal+Smartphone+Clamp+Pro+Version+MCPIXI) opens widest. I compared five clamps for iPhone and Android phones on maker specs, retailer listings and owner reports; I did not handle them myself.

## Phone mount for tripod comparison by width, build and mounts

**A phone mount for a tripod is only as good as its grip range: the widest phone it holds decides whether your phone fits, case on.** Every pick screws onto the tripod's 1/4"-20 screw, and every one has a cold shoe mount for a microphone or LED light. If your tripod head takes Arca plates, our guide to [what an Arca-Swiss plate is](/blog/what-is-an-arca-swiss-plate-and-do-i-need-one/) explains why the ST-27's base matters.

## How I picked each phone mount for tripod use

**I kept only clamps that at least two independent reviewers or owners name by model, that you can buy on Amazon, and that fit a standard tripod screw.** The ST-27 is Wirecutter's mount pick, and Glyn Dewis and CameraEgg chose it too. Amateur Photographer and CameraEgg named the MCPIXI. Swarmify paired the SmallRig 4382 with its top filming tripod. I dropped the Joby GripTight PRO 2 (Swarmify reports it is off Joby's catalog) and the Glif and Shoulderpod S1 (no Amazon US listing).

> **From our notes:** The TwistGrip spec stopped me: Manfrotto lists a 75 mm maximum opening, while Apple lists the iPhone 17 Pro Max at 78.0 mm. So the best-looking clamp here cannot hold the biggest iPhone even bare. After that I ranked every clamp by its widest opening minus your phone's width, because a mount that fits with no case left over is the one owners end up returning.

## The 5 best phone mounts for a tripod, reviewed

**The right tripod phone mount depends on your tripod's top and your phone's width; each pick below wins one of those situations.** The video in this section walks through the ST-27 on an iPhone.

### ULANZI ST-27 Metal Phone Tripod Mount: best phone mount for tripod overall

The Ulanzi ST-27 is an aluminum phone tripod mount from Ulanzi, and it is the clamp I'd put on almost any tripod. B&H lists a grip of 2.4 to 3.5 in, 123 g, three cold shoes and three 1/4" threads; the base is an Arca-type plate with a 1/4" socket, so it drops into a travel tripod head or screws onto a basic one. A dial locks the 360 degree rotation, so switching between portrait and landscape takes a second. Glyn Dewis found it rock steady in coastal wind, where his old holder never felt safe. The catch, from B&H owners: the spring is so strong it is hard to pull back.

### ULANZI ST-02S Aluminum Phone Tripod Mount: best budget metal phone mount

The Ulanzi ST-02S is an all-metal, spring-loaded clamp from Ulanzi, and my pick when you want metal without paying for the ST-27's extras. It opens from 2.6 to 3.7 in, weighs 83 g, grips with rubber pads and has one cold shoe for a mic. A DPReview veteran calls it very adjustable, with an Arca-style foot. There is no rotating joint: B&H notes one 1/4"-20 hole for vertical and one for horizontal, so switching means unscrewing it.

### Manfrotto Universal Smartphone Clamp, Pro Version (MCPIXI): best phone clamp for wide phones and cases

The Manfrotto MCPIXI is a plastic smartphone clamp from Manfrotto, and the one I'd buy for a Pro Max or Ultra in a thick case. Manfrotto rates it for [phones 60mm to 104mm wide](https://www.manfrotto.com/global-en/pixi-clamp-for-smartphone-with-multiple-attachments-mcpixi/), the widest here, and it weighs 41 g. A spring plus a side lock holds the phone, and two 1/4" threads give portrait or landscape. Amateur Photographer notes it is fully plastic, and CameraEgg says it lacks a rotating head, so I'd mount it on a ball head for odd angles.

### SMALLRIG Universal Phone Holder Mount with Cold Shoe 4382: best phone mount for a camera rig

The [SmallRig 4382](amzn:SMALLRIG+universal+phone+holder+mount+with+cold+shoe+4382) is a metal phone holder from SmallRig, and I'd pick it if your phone also rides on a camera or cage. Its base takes a 1/4" screw or slides into a cold shoe, two hinges tilt it, and the phone rotates 360 degrees. B&H lists 2.5 to 3.5 in, 73 g and a 1.1 lb (500 g) load. Owners praise the firm metal grip, but one says the spring can crack a screen protector's edge and another found it short for an iPhone 17 Pro in a case.

## Which phone mount for your tripod should you get?

**Choose by what sits on top of your tripod first, then by how wide your phone is in its case.**

- Arca-type tripod head, or you want a mic and a light: Ulanzi ST-27.
- Plain 1/4" screw and a tight budget: Ulanzi ST-02S.
- Pro Max or Ultra in a thick case: Manfrotto MCPIXI.
- Phone also goes on a camera cage or cold shoe: SmallRig 4382.
- Standard-size phone and pocket carry: Manfrotto TwistGrip.

## Phone mount width vs your phone and case

**An iPhone 17 Pro Max is [78.0 mm wide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/125091) bare, so a tripod phone mount needs several millimeters of spare opening on top of that for a case.** Here is the margin each pick leaves:

- MCPIXI (104 mm): about 26 mm spare, room for a rugged case.
- ST-02S (3.7 in, 94 mm): about 16 mm spare.
- ST-27 and SmallRig 4382 (3.5 in, 88.9 mm): about 11 mm, roughly 5 mm per side, so slim cases only.
- TwistGrip (75 mm): 3 mm short of a bare 17 Pro Max.

Measure your cased phone; the clamp's maximum opening matters, not the phone list on the box.

## How to mount a phone on a camera tripod

**To mount a phone on a camera tripod, screw the clamp onto the tripod's 1/4"-20 screw, tighten it, then load the phone and lock the jaws.** If you are unsure your tripod qualifies, see [can you use any tripod for a camera](/blog/can-you-use-any-tripod-for-a-camera/).

1. Remove the tripod's quick release plate, or keep an Arca-Swiss quick release head and clamp a mount like the ST-27 straight into it.
2. Thread the clamp's base onto the 1/4"-20 screw thread until it is snug; the same screw sits on a selfie stick or light stand.
3. Open the jaws, center the phone with the camera bump clear, and let the spring close.
4. Tighten any locking knob and set portrait or landscape.
5. Tug the phone gently before you walk away.

## Phone tripod mount problems owners report, and the fix

**Most phone tripod mount complaints come down to fit, pressed buttons, wind and stiff springs.**

- **Clamp too narrow for a Pro Max:** check the maximum opening against your cased phone; pick the MCPIXI or ST-02S for thick cases.
- **Jaws pressing Camera Control or volume:** an r/iphone owner hunted for a mount that would not press the Camera Control button. Grip the phone slightly off-center so the jaws sit between buttons, then test them.
- **Plastic clamp not trusted in wind:** a metal clamp with a lock, such as the ST-27, is what Glyn Dewis switched to; our guide on [stopping tripod shake in wind](/blog/how-to-stop-tripod-shake-in-wind/) covers the legs.
- **Spring too strong:** open the jaws with both hands and avoid gripping right on a screen protector's edge.

If you would rather snap the phone on by magnet, a MagSafe mount works for calm indoor live streaming, but a locking clamp is safer in wind. For a complete phone tripod with a remote, see our [phone tripods for vlogging](/blog/best-phone-tripod-for-vlogging-with-remote/).
