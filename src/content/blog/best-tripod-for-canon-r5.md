## How I picked each Canon R5 tripod

**A Canon R5 tripod made this list only when two or more independent guides or R5 owner threads recommend that exact model and Amazon US has it in stock.** The Tortoise 35C comes from a CameraEgg R5 Mark II guide and a DPReview R5 thread, the Mach3 TMA38CL from the same DPReview thread and a CameraEgg R5 C guide, the LS-324C from two R5 owners on forums, and the AM-284 from Packapop and a Canon Rumors R5 Mark II thread. The Gitzo Mountaineer GT2542 came up only once, so it missed my two-source rule.

> **From our notes:** The Tortoise 35C's 18.4 mm bottom tube is what moved it to the top of my R5 list. I had marked it down for five leg sections, because a DPReview R5 reply warns that thin five-section legs end near 10 mm. Then I read Benro's spec sheet: that tube nearly matches the 18.6 mm bottom of the four-section SIRUI. So I stopped counting sections and compared tube sizes, then height without a column. That order decided the rest.

## The 5 best tripods for the Canon R5, reviewed

**Each Canon R5 tripod below fits one job, from 45 MP landscapes to the RF 100-500 to 8K video.**

### Benro Tortoise 35C with GX35 Ball Head: best all-round tripod for the R5

The Benro Tortoise 35C with GX35 Ball Head is a columnless five-section carbon fiber tripod from Benro, and the [Tortoise 35C kit](amzn:Benro+Tortoise+Columnless+Carbon+Fiber+Three+Series+Tripod+GX35+TTOR35CGX35) is my all-round pick for an R5 with a 24-70 F2.8 or 70-200 F2.8. It reaches 61.22 in with no column to catch wind, so the head sits on the leg spider where it is stiffest. Benro rates it to 15 kg, and the GX35 has a 40 mm ball, dual panning and an Arca-style plate. One owner-reviewer moved from a Manfrotto 055 and found it steady with a long telephoto at about 1.9 kg, but he worries grit could reach the twist locks over time. I'd take it as the one R5 tripod for 45 MP stills and locked-off 8K.

### Benro Mach3 TMA38CL: best tall tripod for the RF 100-500

The Benro Mach3 TMA38CL is a four-section 9X carbon fiber tripod from Benro, sold as legs only, and the [Mach3 TMA38CL](amzn:Benro+Mach3+TMA38CL) is the one I'd put under an R5 with the RF 100-500, the heaviest telephoto lens in this guide. It reaches 69.7 in with the column up and is rated to 16 kg, the highest here. A DPReview R5 thread reply says the difference from an aluminum Manfrotto was distinct, and one long-term owner says it still works perfectly after three and a half years, though mud once got into his twist locks. I'd add a gimbal or a 40 mm-class ball head; the short column in the box gets it lower.

## How to set up a Canon R5 tripod for 45 MP stills and 8K video

**A Canon R5 tripod gives sharp 45 MP files when the column stays down, the heavy lens sits over the legs and the head is locked.**

1. Extend the thickest legs and sections first and leave the thinnest ones in.
2. Keep the center column down; raise legs, not the column.
3. Mount the RF 70-200 or 100-500 by its tripod ring, not the camera.
4. Set the ball head friction before you loosen it, so the head can't creep or let the lens flop.
5. Fire with the 2-second timer or a remote for long exposures.
6. For 8K pans, level the 75 mm bowl first, then set the fluid head drag.

## Canon R5 tripod problems owners run into, and the fix

**Most Canon R5 tripod complaints come from thin leg sections, a creeping head, wind or salt, not from the camera.**

- **Thin travel legs.** A DPReview R5 reply says "usually the thinnest leg sections are around 10mm diameter and simply not stable enough for a heavy-ish FF camera with large zoom". The three stills picks with published tube sizes end at 18.4 mm or more; our [4 vs 5 section tripod guide](/blog/4-section-vs-5-section-tripod/) explains the trade.
- **Load ratings mislead.** The same reply advises to "completely ignore the stated load ratings - there is no standard". That is why I compared tubes and height.
- **Flimsy in wind.** A CameraEgg forum R5 owner with an RF 70-200 said "the whole thing just felt so flimsy in the wind and I ended up with blurry shots". Go columnless, and lower.
- **A creeping ball head.** A Canon Rumors reply warns "A 70-200mm can get a bit front-heavy". Use the lens ring and a 40 mm-class ball like the GX35 or LH-40.
- **Manfrotto clamps.** The DPReview reply notes Manfrotto's own clamp is "NOT compatible with Arca Swiss", so an R5 L-bracket or Arca-Swiss plate won't fit; the Tortoise and Leofoto kits ship with Arca-type clamps, and the two legs-only picks take any Arca head.
- **Salt spray.** An R5 owner on the CameraEgg forum says "you have to rinse the leg locks in the shower every single night or they will start grinding and eventually seize up".

For a windy coast and a 45 MP long exposure, I'd carry the LS-324CX; for everything else, the Tortoise 35C.
