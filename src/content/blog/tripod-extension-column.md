A tripod extension column is a screw-on tube that sits between the tripod's top and the head and adds roughly 12 to 16 inches of height. For a tall photographer it is worth it when the legs stop a few inches short of eye level and the camera is light, but it shakes like a raised center column, so it never replaces legs that reach eye level on their own.

Tall readers ask us this when they would rather add height than replace a good tripod. If you are still choosing, our guide to the [best tripods for tall photographers over 6 feet](/blog/best-tripod-for-tall-photographers-over-6-feet/) compares models by legs-only height.

## How a tripod extension column adds height

**An extension column for a tripod threads onto the 1/4"-20 or 3/8"-16 stud on your tripod top, and the head screws onto the column, so all the extra height comes from one carbon fiber extension tube.** Two real examples:

| Spec | Sunwayfoto ET-01 | Benro carbon extension column |
|---|---|---|
| Extra height | up to [12 in](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/1301981-REG/sunwayfoto_et_01_carbon_fiber_tripod_extension.html) | column extends to 16 in (maker) |
| Collapsed length | 7.4 in | 7.9 in |
| Sections | 2, twist lock | 3 |
| Weight | 4.1 oz | 0.48 lb (#3 size) |
| Load rating | 22 lb | not listed |
| Fit | 1/4"-20, adapts to 3/8"-16 | Benro Series 0, 1, 2 or 3 |

## Does a tripod extension column reduce stability?

**Yes, a tripod extension column reduces stability because it raises the camera above the apex where the legs meet, adding torque, so every gust and shutter press becomes a bigger, longer vibration.** Built-in or screw-on, the physics are the same.

Tester The Center Column measured it. On a Benro tripod with a medium-format camera, vibrations decayed [four times slower](https://thecentercolumn.com/2021/02/01/why-center-columns-reduce-tripod-stability/) with the column fully extended, and simply fitting a column cost one tripod roughly 15% of its stiffness. A carbon column also lost [65% of its side-to-side stiffness](https://thecentercolumn.com/2018/04/03/carbon-fiber-vs-aluminum-center-column/) at full height, against 29% for an aluminum one.

Rokslide member briscoetab, whose Leofoto has a screw-on extension column, puts it plainly: "only extend it an inch or two probably will have little effect as far as stability but extend it very far and it will", especially in wind.

> **From our notes:** The Sunwayfoto ET-01's 4.1-ounce weight against its 22 lb rating made us run the numbers. A load rating says the tube won't fail; it says nothing about how long a 12-inch lever keeps ringing. In The Center Column's test, a fully raised column took about five seconds to settle under a mid-size zoom. So we judge any extension column by how few inches we need from it.

## When a tripod extension column makes sense for tall photographers

**An add-on column makes sense when your viewfinder sits about 3 to 8 inches below eye level, your kit is light, and the air is calm.** Eye level sits about 4 to 5 inches below the top of your head, and a head plus camera add about 5 to 8 inches to the legs-only height.

For a 6'2" photographer (eye level about 69 to 70 inches) on a Manfrotto MT055XPRO3, which B&H lists at 55 inches with the column down, the viewfinder lands at 60 to 63 inches: 6 to 10 inches short. An extension column covers that, but only near full extension, where it shakes most.

- **Within 3 inches:** raise the built-in column a little; our [center column rules for landscapes](/blog/tripod-center-column-up-or-down-for-landscapes/) cap it at about six inches.
- **3 to 8 inches short:** an extension column suits light kit, glassing and occasional standing shots.
- **More than 8 inches, heavy lenses or wind:** taller legs, as our [tall tripod vs regular tripod](/blog/tall-tripod-vs-regular-tripod/) comparison explains.

Rokslide member pyrotechnic added a column to his RRS tripod because "it wasn't quite high enough for me to comfortably use standing", and noticed no major stability issues while glassing with binoculars. That is the forum complaint "a travel pod will be shorter requiring you to bend over to look through the VF", solved with a few inches.

## How to fit a tripod extension column safely

**Fit the column by matching the thread, checking the load and extending it last.**

1. Match the stud on your tripod top (1/4"-20 or 3/8"-16), using the bushing adapter if one is included.
2. Check the load capacity against camera, lens and tripod head together.
3. Extend the tripod legs first, thickest sections before thin ones.
4. Pull out only the column you need and lock every section.
5. Hang a bag low, spread the legs and fire with a self-timer or remote release.

I would leave it off for long telephoto lenses and long exposures in wind; if that is most of your shooting, the extra inches belong in the legs.
