## How to choose a travel tripod

**A good travel tripod is the lightest, shortest-folding tripod that still holds your real camera and lens steady at a comfortable working height.** Every travel tripod is a compromise between packed size and stability, so the trick is knowing which numbers matter for the way you travel.

### What to look for

Start with three numbers from the spec sheet, in this order:

1. **Load capacity against your gear.** Add up the published weights of your body, lens, plate and anything else on the camera. A rated load is the maximum the maker claims, not a comfortable working load, so we suggest a tripod rated for about double your kit.
2. **Folded length against your bag.** Measure the inside of your carry-on or the side pocket of your daypack, then compare it with the folded length including the head. Folded width matters too: a slim fold slides into a side pocket, a bulky one eats a compartment.
3. **Height with the center column down.** This is the tripod's stable working height. The headline maximum usually includes a fully raised column, which puts the camera on a single thin tube.

### Key specs and trade-offs

- **Leg sections.** Five sections fold shorter than four, but mean more locks to open on every setup and thinner bottom tubes.
- **Twist vs lever locks.** Twist locks are slim and quick once you get the knack; lever (flip) locks show at a glance whether each section is clamped and are easier with gloves. Both need rinsing after sand or salt water.
- **Carbon fiber vs aluminum.** Carbon is lighter and damps vibration faster; aluminum costs less and weighs more. The same design in both materials often differs by a few hundred grams.
- **Head and plate.** Most travel kits ship with a small ball head. An Arca-Swiss compatible clamp lets you share plates and L-brackets with other tripods.

### Who needs what

A light APS-C or compact mirrorless kit can live on a very short, very light tripod with a modest load rating. A full-frame body with a 70-200mm f/2.8 needs a stiffer tripod with real headroom, even if it folds a little longer. ### Mistakes to avoid

- **Buying on maximum height alone.** Compare the column-down figure instead.
- **Ignoring the head's rating.** Use the lower of the legs' and the head's load ratings.
- **Forgetting the head in the folded length.** Some tripods fold around the head, others don't.
- **Loading to the limit.** A tripod at its rated maximum droops and shakes, especially in wind.
- **Raising the column in wind.** Lower the whole tripod and hang your bag from the hook if there is one.
