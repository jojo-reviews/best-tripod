## How to choose a tripod head

**Choose a tripod head by plate system first, then by how you move the camera: a ball head for speed and low weight, a pan-tilt or geared head for precise, one-axis-at-a-time framing, and a fluid head for video.** The head is the part of the tripod you touch on every shot, so it shapes how the whole support feels.

### What to look for

- **Plate system.** Arca-Swiss is an open dovetail standard, so plates and L-brackets from many brands fit any Arca clamp. Proprietary plates only fit matching clamps, which usually means buying extra plates from the same maker.
- **Rated load against your kit.** Add up your camera, lens and plate weights and leave a wide margin; a head near its limit is more likely to droop.
- **Friction control.** A separate tension knob lets the camera stay where you leave it before you lock, which reduces droop on a ball head.
- **Panning base.** A separate pan lock lets you level once and pan for panoramas without loosening the ball.
- **Weight and size.** Heads around half a kilo suit travel kits; heavier heads with levers or gear knobs are better for car-based locations.

### Key specs and trade-offs

- **Ball heads** are quick and compact, and one knob frees every axis. The downside is that loosening the ball can tip a carefully set horizon, and some heads sag slightly when locked.
- **Pan-tilt (3-way) heads** use separate controls for pan, tilt and portrait, so a level horizon stays level. They are bulkier, and in portrait mode they tip the camera off center unless you use an L-bracket.
- **Geared heads** move each axis in small, repeatable steps, which suits panoramas, focus stacks and filter work but is slow when light changes fast.
- **Fluid heads** add damped drag for smooth video pans; they are bulkier than photo heads.

### Who needs what

Hikers and travelers usually end up on a compact Arca-Swiss ball head, often adding a leveling base or panning clamp for panoramas. ### Mistakes to avoid

- **Mixing plate systems.** Pick one standard for all your tripods and brackets.
- **Buying for the headline load.** Use the lower of the head's and the legs' ratings.
- **Skipping friction control.** Without it, heavy lenses flop when you loosen the ball.
- **Ignoring portrait orientation.** An L-bracket on an Arca clamp keeps the camera centered over the tripod.
- **Using a ball head for regular video.** Pans start and stop with a jerk; a fluid head is the right tool.
