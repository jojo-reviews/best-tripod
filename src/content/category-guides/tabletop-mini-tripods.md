## How to choose a mini or tabletop tripod

**A good mini tripod holds your camera or phone steady on a desk or table, reaches a useful height once raised, and attaches in a way that suits your gear.** Mini tripods are small and inexpensive, which makes it easy to overlook the handful of specs that decide whether one actually works.

### What to look for

- **Working height.** Most tabletop tripods sit somewhere between a few inches and about a foot and a half tall. For video calls, that is usually below seated eye level, so plan to raise the tripod on books or a box rather than tilting the camera up.
- **Load capacity.** Weigh your camera with its lens, cage and microphone and compare it with the lowest published rating. Some mini tripods rate their head differently in vertical orientation.
- **Mount.** Most use the standard 1/4 in-20 thread found on cameras and many webcams. Phones need a separate clamp, and an Arca-type plate helps if you move a camera between supports.
- **Adjustment.** Fixed legs are simplest; telescopic arms and adjustable leg angles add height and flexibility.

### Key specs and trade-offs

- **Fixed vs extendable.** A fixed mini tripod is solid and simple but stays low; an extendable one gains height and gets less steady as it extends.
- **Rigid vs flexible legs.** Rigid legs are steadier on a flat desk; flexible legs can wrap around a monitor arm or railing but give up some stability.
- **Weight.** A heavier, all-metal mini tripod resists bumps better; a lighter one packs into a laptop bag more easily.

### Who needs what

A webcam on a desk needs little more than a stable, low stand with a standard thread. A phone needs a clamp and enough height to reach eye level once raised. A mirrorless camera used as a webcam needs the strongest head of the bunch and a slack cable run so the USB or HDMI lead doesn't tug the camera. ### Mistakes to avoid

- **Aiming up from the desk surface.** A camera tilted up from below gives an unflattering under-chin view; raise the whole tripod instead.
- **Trusting the headline load.** Plan around the lower, more cautious figure.
- **Forgetting the phone clamp.** Many camera mini tripods don't include one.
- **Letting the tripod skate.** Rubber feet or a mouse mat underneath help when you bump the desk.
- **Ignoring lighting.** Face a window or add a small light; a steady camera can't fix a dark picture.
