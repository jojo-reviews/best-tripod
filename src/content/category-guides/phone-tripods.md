## How to choose a phone tripod

**The right phone tripod gets your phone to the height you film at, holds it securely in its case, and lets you start and stop recording without touching the screen.** Phone tripods look alike in photos, but they split into very different tools once you check height, mount and remote.

### What to look for

- **Height.** Extendable stick tripods reach standing height; flexible or tabletop tripods stay low and need a table, railing or riser. Decide where you film before anything else.
- **Phone mount.** A spring clamp grips the sides of the phone, so its listed width limits which phones and cases fit. Magnetic mounts skip the clamp but depend on a compatible phone or an adhesive metal ring.
- **Remote.** A Bluetooth shutter remote lets you start a clip from where you stand. Check the listed range, how it is powered (rechargeable or replaceable battery), and whether it docks somewhere on the tripod so it doesn't get lost.
- **Orientation.** A ball head or rotating holder lets you switch between portrait for short-form video and landscape for long-form.
- **Thread.** A 1/4 in-20 thread or adapter lets the same tripod carry a small camera or light.

### Key specs and trade-offs

Tall selfie-stick tripods double as handheld sticks for walking shots, but a slim top section can wobble at full extension, especially with a light and microphone on top. Flexible tripods pack small and grip odd surfaces, but they never reach eye level by themselves. Some phone tripods publish no load rating at all, so keep extra accessories light and treat any rating as a ceiling.

### Who needs what

Vloggers who film standing up want an extendable tripod with a remote and a secure mount. Creators who film at a desk or wrap a tripod around a railing are better served by a flexible or tabletop design. ### Mistakes to avoid

- **Measuring the phone without its case.** Clamp width is the limit that matters, and a large phone in a thick case can exceed it.
- **Filming at full extension in wind.** Collapse the top section or two and stand a little farther back instead.
- **Trusting the remote at its maximum range.** Owner reports of dropped connections at longer distances suggest staying well inside it.
- **Skipping a test clip.** Pair the remote, record a few seconds and play it back before you walk away from the tripod.
- **Pointing the phone up from below.** Raise the tripod to eye level instead; a camera tilted up gives an unflattering angle.

When you outgrow a phone and move to a mirrorless camera, the same height and stability logic applies, with a proper load rating and plate system added on top.
